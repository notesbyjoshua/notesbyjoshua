## Course roadmap

AP Calculus is built around three connected ideas:

- **Limits** describe what functions approach.
- **Derivatives** describe instantaneous change.
- **Integrals** describe accumulation.

AB focuses on limits, derivatives, integrals, differential equations, and applications. BC includes all of AB plus additional techniques with parametric, polar, and vector-valued functions, and infinite series.

When studying, try to connect each unit to at least two representations:

- formulas,
- graphs,
- tables,
- verbal/contextual descriptions.

Most AP questions are not just asking whether you can compute. They are asking whether you can justify why a theorem applies, interpret a derivative or integral in context, and move between representations without losing units or conditions.

These notes are organized by AP unit, but some deeper explanations follow the usual Calculus I/II flow: limits, derivatives, derivative applications, integrals, integration techniques, integral applications, parametric/polar curves, and sequences/series. BC-only material stops at sequences and series here; vector and 3D-coordinate topics belong outside the AP Calculus AB/BC scope.
